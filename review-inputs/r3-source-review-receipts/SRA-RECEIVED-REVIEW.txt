### 3.3 Modified-F moment reasoning

Under the equal-mean null, write y_i=x_i-mu and
V=diag(sigma_i^2/n_i). Then
A=sum(n_i*y_i^2)-(sum(n_i*y_i))^2/N. Taking expectations directly gives
E(A)=sum(sigma_i^2)-sum(n_i*sigma_i^2)/N=E(D).
Independence and the normal-model variance of each sample variance give
Var(D)=2 sum((1-n_i/N)^2*sigma_i^4/(n_i-1)).

Matching D to a scaled chi-square therefore gives f=2 E(D)^2/Var(D);
substitution of observed variances produces exactly the printed f_B.
The numerator is generally a weighted normal quadratic form with unequal
nonzero eigenvalues. Matching denominator moments does not make it an exact
chi-square numerator or make the ratio an exact F pivot. The report's
approximate-versus-exact distinction is mathematically sound.

### 3.4 Two-group and balanced reductions

For a=s_1^2/n_1, b=s_2^2/n_2 and delta=x_1-x_2:
A=(n_1*n_2/N)*delta^2 and D=(n_1*n_2/N)*(a+b). Consequently
F-star=delta^2/(a+b). Inverse-variance weighted dispersion gives the same Q;
the Welch correction vanishes for g=2, hence W=Q.
Both denominator degrees of freedom reduce to
`(a+b)^2/[a^2/(n_1-1)+b^2/(n_2-1)]`.

For equal group sizes n, D=(g-1)*sum(s_i^2)/g; substitution proves that
F-star's statistic equals ordinary ANOVA F. Its denominator df is
`(n-1)*(sum(s_i^2))^2/sum(s_i^4)`, at most g(n-1), with equality only when
the observed variances are equal. The distinction in the report is correct.
