## Bounded source findings and impact

1. **Property I, pp.519-521:** retain every actually superior process with
   probability at least 1-alpha. This is not a guarantee of correctly classifying
   all processes or proving every retained process superior.
2. **Rules A/B, pp.521-524:** known standard, independent statistics from a common
   stochastically ordered family. B excludes in sequence and stops at the first
   non-exclusion. Continuous and discrete calibration differ. Theorem 1 supplies
   a retention proof under these premises. This is original historical content,
   not the accepted pooled treatment-minus-control joint-t model.
3. **Comparison metrics, pp.524-528:** superior retention, inferior exclusion and
   complete classification are different probabilities. Printed p.524 reverses
   the stated A/B preference while immediately giving P_I(B)>P_I(A); the following
   event inclusion supports B for that metric. Record the inconsistency; do not
   silently repair the source or infer universal complete-classification dominance.
4. **Exponential-ratio rules, pp.529-531:** known-standard equal-count A1/B1 use
   F distributions. Unknown-standard A2/B2 share a random control denominator;
   exact joint calibration, a conservative marginal replacement and a further
   large-sample approximation are distinct. No certified numerical oracle follows.
5. **Unequal counts, pp.532-534:** known-standard B3 retains distribution labels
   in subset-dependent thresholds. Unknown-standard unequal counts instead lead
   to the separate single-step Rule C. Neither is the already accepted 1991
   unequal-size pooled joint-t step-down construction.
6. **Lineage, pp.520/534-535:** Dunnett 1955 and Gupta-Sobel antecedents are named.
   A later citation to Naik establishes historical connection, not identical
   models, adjusted-p recursions, compatible intervals or exhaustive priority.

The exact rule/calibration and claim-to-page ledger are retained in the original
