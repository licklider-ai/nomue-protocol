## Bounded claim-to-page ledger

1. **Original target and guarantee -- pp.519-521, PDF 2-4.** The paper divides processes into selected-as-superior and selected-as-inferior subsets. Property I requires probability at least 1-alpha that the selected-as-superior subset contains all actually superior processes. It does not assert probability at least 1-alpha of correct classification of every process, or that every retained process is superior. Section 2 calls theta_i >= theta_0 superior, with theta_0 known. The exponential-ratio application uses the opposite preference: theta_i = lambda_i/mu_i <= theta_0 is superior (p.520 and p.529). Direction is part of the procedure, not an interchangeable label.

2. **Section 2 premises -- pp.521-522, PDF 4-5.** Independent statistics T_i, a common distribution family when their parameters coincide, and stochastic increase with theta_i; equal sample sizes are the stated starting setting. Equation (1) gives equality of the distributions at a common parameter; equation (2) gives the stochastic inequality. The normal example on p.522 uses theta_i = mu_i/sigma_i and T_i = sqrt(n) times sample mean divided by that sample's standard deviation. It is not the later pooled-variance treatment-minus-random-control statistic.

3. **Rules A/B and exact boundaries -- pp.522-523, PDF 5-6.** A excludes to the inferior subset when T_i < a^(p). For continuous distributions, P_theta0[T_i >= a^(ell)] = (1-alpha)^(1/ell). B orders T descending and starts at the smallest, comparing T^(ell) to a^(ell) for ell=p downward. It excludes while T^(ell) < a^(ell), and stops at the first T^(ell) >= a^(ell), retaining that and the remaining larger values. The discrete prescription replaces the calibration equality with a conservative inequality and chooses the largest integer threshold. It should not be silently treated as the continuous equality calibration.

4. **Theorem 1 -- pp.523-524, PDF 6-7.** For q truly superior processes, the event that all their statistics are at least a^(q) is contained in retention of them all. Independence, stochastic ordering, and calibration give probability at least 1-alpha. This is an actual printed argument, not a citation-only claim. Review inference: identifying H_i with theta_i >= theta_0 and rejection with inferior selection turns this bound into strong familywise false-exclusion control. This translation does not provide the joint-t model used by the accepted 1991 characterization.

5. **Comparison metrics -- pp.524-528, PDF 7-11; Fig.1 and Table I inspected.** The source separates P_S, P_I, and P_C (all superior correct, all inferior correct, all processes correct). Rule B excludes a superset of the processes excluded by A. Greater ability to exclude inferiors does not imply greater probability of complete correct classification in every configuration. The discussion explicitly gives configurations favoring A for P_C, and other comparisons are qualified by family/configuration or computed examples. No universal P_C dominance should be imported. The source-local wording issue below applies.

6. **Known standard, equal counts, ratio application -- pp.529-530, PDF 12-13, Section 3.1.** Independent exponential observations with means lambda_i and mu_i; F_i is the ratio of their sample means, with F_i/theta_i distributed as F(2m,2n). A1 excludes for F_i > v_(p). B1 sorts F ascending, starts with the largest, excludes above v_(ell), and stops at F_(ell) <= v_(ell). Calibration is P[F(2m,2n) <= v_(ell)/theta_0] = (1-alpha)^(1/ell). The printed three-process example produces different selected subsets for A and B. Its printed numbers were read, not independently recomputed.

7. **Unknown standard, equal treatment counts -- pp.530-531, PDF 13-14, Section 3.2.** Additional independent exponential control samples give F_0; Z_i=F_i/F_0 share that denominator. A2/B2 replace F_i and v_(ell) by Z_i and w_(ell). Exact calibration uses the joint event that all ell Z_i are <= w_(ell), at boundary theta_i=theta_0, with probability 1-alpha. Theorem 2 conditions on F_0 and integrates a product of conditional probabilities, giving the Property-I bound. Shared control therefore is addressed in this original, but through ratio-F/exponential distributions, not through pooled multivariate t.

8. **Exact versus conservative/approximate quantities -- p.531, PDF 14.** The source gives alternative w^0_(ell) from P[F(2m,2n)/F(2m_0,2n_0) <= w^0_(ell)] = (1-alpha)^(1/ell), with independent F variates. It invokes Jensen to preserve Property I. Review inference: this marginal equation is a conservative replacement for the joint calibration because the joint probability is an expectation of a power, rather than a power of its expectation. The further large-sample normal approximation to log(chi-square/df) is another approximation; no certified numerical error bound is supplied by this reading. Do not collapse all three objects into an exact threshold.

9. **Known standard, unequal counts -- pp.532-533, PDF 15-16, Section 3.3.** The independent F_i have different degrees of freedom. A3 uses the full-family product calibration. B3 orders the observations, then determines thresholds for that arrangement using the labels/degrees of freedom of the remaining subset; this is not an exchangeable threshold depending on subset size alone. The proof uses the first erroneous exclusion: the remaining subset contains all q truly superior processes, so its threshold is at least the fixed true-subset threshold. Preserve distribution labels when translating the displayed ranked notation; do not reinterpret its calibration as the distribution of sorted random variables. This labelled-subset interpretation is the reviewer reading of the rule and proof, not an assertion that the paper uses modern notation perfectly.

10. **Unknown standard, unequal counts -- pp.533-534, PDF 16-17, Section 3.4.** The paper explicitly notes difficulty obtaining A/B constants here and instead proposes Rule C. It compares each Z_i to its own w'_(i), defined by a marginal ratio of independent F variates with probability (1-alpha)^(1/p). This is a distinct single-step rule, not a supplied unequal-count joint-t step-down algorithm. The proof uses conditional independence and a product lower bound attributed to Kimball (1951). Kimball's original proof was not acquired/read here.

11. **Reference lineage -- pp.520, 534-535, PDF 3, 17-18.** The original discusses antecedents including Paulson, Dunnett, Gupta-Sobel and Gupta, and lists Dunnett 1955 and Gupta-Sobel 1958. The original and cover independently fix Naik's bibliographic identity. The accepted historical 1991 report records its citation to Naik. These facts support a historical relationship, not an exhaustive priority claim, proof of all later attributions, or exact equivalence of formulations. Referenced originals were not independently acquired in this pass.

## Concrete conflict and comparison with accepted MTO-02

**Source-local wording inconsistency, p.524:** after specifying that the larger P_I is preferred, the page says A is preferable to B while immediately stating P_I(B)>P_I(A). The event inclusion on p.525 supports B under that metric. I visually confirmed the line at 180 dpi. Treat the preference word/order as inconsistent with the criterion and following argument; do not quote it as a supported assertion of A's greater inferior-selection probability. This is not a defect in Theorem 1's Property-I proof and does not contradict a relied-upon accepted 1991 claim.

The accepted MTO-02 record concerns independent normal groups, common unknown variance, an independent pooled chi-square scale, a specified control, unequal group sizes, labelled remaining-subset maximum-t calibration, and suffix-maximum adjusted p-values. Naik's Section 2 uses independent same-family scalar statistics against a known standard; its detailed Section 3 variants use exponential mean ratios and F laws. Naik supplies relevant stopped elimination and subset-calibration arguments, including a shared-control case and a label-sensitive unequal-count case. Those are historical and structural connections. They do not establish equivalence under the same premises because the actual models, estimands, and calibrations differ.

This review found no presentation in Naik of the accepted 1991 pooled joint-t construction, its adjusted-p recursion, its two-sided absolute-statistic construction, or compatible confidence intervals. It provides no basis to expand MTO-02 to heteroscedastic mean comparisons, cross-endpoint families, or an average-correlation calibration. Nor does its normal example with different sigma_i establish such a mean-comparison guarantee: its target is standardized mu_i/sigma_i with a known standard.

The Section 3.4 alternative is a genuine variant in the original paper, but it is outside the already accepted normal-means construction. No concrete material in-scope contradiction requiring reversal of SR-J was demonstrated. No claim about MTO-03, MCB-01, or Holm changes as a result of this reading.
