## Zimmerman 1997: claim-by-claim result

- **Z97-1, supported with pinpoint correction.** Article p.351, section *Paired Data and Nonindependence of Observations*, specifies normal deviates, induced correlations from -0.50 to +0.50, n from 10 to 80, one- and two-tailed alpha .05 tests, and 10,000 replications per point. Its explicitly illustrative scope remains essential. Table 1 is on article p.356 (PDF p.9), not within the earlier review's stated pp.351-355 coverage. Figures 1 and 2 are pp.353 and 354. No arbitrary clustering or nonnormal-data theorem follows.
- **Z97-2, supported as a bounded interpretation.** Article pp.349-350 describe repeated subjects and matching; pp.357-359 discuss incidental and hidden correspondence; Table 2 on p.358 begins from a specified hidden pairing. The inference that a verifier cannot discover the design from column correlation is the review's application of these distinctions, not a Protocol rule written by Zimmerman. The paper itself discusses near-zero correlation as strong independence evidence within its setting (p.359); it does not establish a generally valid sample-correlation classifier. Preserve the distinction between a known design relation, population dependence, and an observed sample statistic.
- **Z97-3, supported; missing pinpoint supplied.** Article p.352, Figure 1 upper panel p.353, Table 1 p.356, and conclusions p.357. The paired test remains around .05; negative pairwise correlation elevates independent-test rejection and positive pairwise correlation depresses it. This is cross-pairing behavior in the studied setup, not a universal positive within-cluster inflation claim. The p.357 discussion explicitly treats distortion in both directions.
- **Z97-4, supported with cautious phrasing; missing pinpoint supplied.** Article pp.359-360, concluding comparison of two different design questions. The .30-.40 range is a tentative recommendation for deliberate matching at studied sample sizes; p.359 also assumes near-zero correlation before matching. The much lower .05-.15 discussion concerns already paired observations. These are not exact crossover constants, admissibility thresholds, or proof that pairing can be selected by a measured correlation. Prefer 'the paper tentatively recommends' over an unqualified 'would need'.
- **Z97-5, supported; missing pinpoint supplied.** Article p.358, Table 2 and surrounding example, followed by the third conclusion on p.359. The example specifies population correlation .100 and sample correlation .139 after revealing the hidden correspondence; the paper's .10/.15 warning is on p.359. It is not a procedure for finding pairs from observed values. Keep the simulation and known-correspondence qualifications.

A source-internal caution: p.352 calls the Type I error plot an upper section of Figure 2, although the actual plot is Figure 1 on p.353. That page also describes 20 pairs while subsequently giving 9 and 18 degrees of freedom. These are inconsistencies in the supplied article, not newly discovered flaws in R5. Pinpoints should identify the actual figure/caption; no numerical implementation or silently repaired source text is authorized. The bounded direction-of-distortion claims do not depend on resolving those inconsistencies.

Disposition: the prior narrowed content conclusion is retained. No materially false bounded claim was found. Correct Table 1's page, add the missing pinpoints, and preserve the tentative nature and limited scope of the matching ranges.

## Zimmerman 2004: claim-by-claim result

- **Z04-1, supported, with an important reproducibility qualification.** Article p.174, section 2 *Method*, continuing at p.175: reject the variance pretest and use Welch-Satterthwaite; otherwise use pooled Student. Tests are nondirectional. The paper specifically describes its named Levene test as a two-sample Student t test on squared deviations from sample means. Record that definition when stating the exact studied procedure; 'Levene' alone should not imply equivalence to every implementation or variant carrying that name. This review does not substitute another algorithm or adjudicate the author's naming.
- **Z04-2, supported; missing pinpoints supplied.** Article p.174 gives independent normal samples, population standard-deviation ratios 1-3, total n of 30 or 60, n1/n2 from .2 to 5, 50,000 replications and the 200,000-replication Table 2 exception. Article pp.174-175 give .01/.05 levels and Table 3's exception; Table 3 on p.178 varies the preliminary alpha through .01, .02, .05, .10 and .20 while holding the location alpha fixed. These are simulation-design statements, not universal method-selection conditions.
- **Z04-3, supported if 'near nominal' is approximate.** Article pp.175-176, Table 1 p.175, Figure 1 p.177, Table 2 p.178, and discussion pp.178-179. They support distorted compound error rates and distortion under conditioning, with dependence on imbalance, variance ratio and pretest alpha. 'Near' is not exact: Table 1 includes unconditional Welch .016 at nominal .01 and .058 at nominal .05 for the 25/5 equal-variance case. Table 2's conditional Welch errors are distinct from unconditional Welch's and from the full compound procedure. Do not collapse those three quantities. The claim about slight variance inequality is a contextual pattern, not monotonicity over every setting.
- **Z04-4, supported; missing pinpoints supplied.** Table 1, p.175, supplies equal-population-variance rows with compound errors near nominal; discussion p.179 supplies the limiting argument where pretest power approaches one and the compound choice approaches Welch. Those observations defeat an 'every choice always invalid' formulation. The author proposes a broader argument about variance pretests, but does not prove a theorem about every outcome-dependent method choice. The earlier bounded replacement claim remains appropriate.
- **Z04-5, supported; missing pinpoints supplied.** Discussion p.179 and section 5 *Practical recommendations*, pp.179-180, especially its closing paragraph. Unconditional separate-variance testing is the author's recommendation, particularly for unequal sample sizes; it is not authority for a general R5 method-selection policy.

Disposition: retain the narrower prior claim and add the exact pretest-definition qualification. No reversal of the bounded content conclusion is warranted. Avoid interpreting approximate error control as exact nominal calibration.
