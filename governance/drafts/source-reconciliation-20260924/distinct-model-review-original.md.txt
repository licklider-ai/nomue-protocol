## 4. Claim-by-claim findings

### 4.1 Naik Property I and rule variants — PASS

Printed p.520 defines Property I as probability at least `1-alpha` that the
selected-as-superior subset contains every actually superior process. Pages
521-524 provide the common-family, independence and stochastic-order premises,
Rules A/B, continuous and discrete calibrations, and Theorem 1's retention bound.
This is not complete correct classification and does not establish that every
retained process is superior. The packet preserves that distinction.

Pages 529-530 treat a known standard and equal counts using independent
exponential-ratio/F statistics. Pages 530-531 treat an unknown standard and equal
treatment counts with a shared random control denominator: the exact joint
calibration, the Jensen-based conservative marginal replacement, and a further
large-sample approximation are visibly different objects. Pages 532-533 treat a
known standard with unequal counts and label/distribution-dependent remaining-set
thresholds. Pages 533-534 explicitly replace the unavailable A/B construction for
an unknown standard with unequal counts by the separate single-step Rule C. The
packet separates all four variants correctly and derives no numerical oracle from
them.

The strongest source-local problem is p.524. After defining the larger `P_I` as
preferred, the text says A is preferable while immediately stating
`P_I(B) > P_I(A)`; the following event inclusion supports B for that metric. The
packet records this inconsistency and confines it to the local preference wording.
It does not affect Theorem 1's retention proof or justify a universal complete-
classification ordering. No repair is required.

### 4.2 Relation to accepted 1991 MTO-02 basis — PASS, with exact equivalence NOT-ASSESSED

Nothing in the inspected Naik pages concretely contradicts the accepted narrowed
MTO-02 characterization. Naik's general Section 2 uses independent scalar
statistics against a known standard, and its detailed Section 3 uses exponential
mean ratios and F laws. The accepted record describes the 1991 construction as
independent normal groups with a common unknown variance, an independent pooled
chi-square scale, labelled remaining-subset joint-t calibration, unequal sizes,
and suffix-maximum adjusted p-values. Naik provides relevant historical and
structural antecedents, but these are different premises and calibrations.

The packet does not claim equivalence, a reconstruction of the 1991 method, or the
absence of every distinct Naik variant. Exact formula-level equivalence or a new
priority/lineage adjudication is NOT-ASSESSED because the 1991 original was not
available in this review. That original would be required only if the project later
wants to assert such a stronger relationship, transfer a Naik proof to the 1991
model, or alter the accepted variant/priority account. It is not needed to accept
the present additive finding that no inspected Naik statement contradicts the
already bounded 1991 basis.

### 4.3 R5 pinpoints and precision repairs — PASS

For Zimmerman 1997, p.351 gives the simulation design and explicitly says it is
illustrative rather than exhaustive. Figure 1 is p.353, Figure 2 p.354, Table 1
p.356, and Table 2 p.358. Pages 359-360 give the `.30-.40` and `.05-.15` ranges as
different setup-dependent questions, not universal cutoffs. The hidden-pairing
example on p.358 starts from a specified correspondence and cannot be turned into
a sample-correlation pairing classifier. The packet's pinpoints and tentative,
limited wording are accurate.

The source itself has p.352 inconsistencies: it refers to an upper section of
Figure 2 where the Type-I plot is actually Figure 1, and says 20 pairs while the
printed 9 and 18 degrees of freedom correspond to a smaller paired/independent
comparison. The packet records this warning and does not derive a numerical
contract from the inconsistent text.

For Zimmerman 2004, p.174 defines the named Levene pretest as a two-sample Student
test on squared deviations from the respective sample means. It is therefore
correct to preserve the exact studied definition rather than silently substitute
every implementation carrying the Levene label. Tables 1-3 and pp.175-179 keep
unconditional Student, unconditional Welch, conditional component errors and the
compound procedure separate. Approximate proximity to nominal is not represented
as equality. Table 1's equal-variance rows and p.179's limiting case, where pretest
power approaches one and the compound procedure approaches Welch, are concrete
counterexamples to an “every outcome-dependent choice always invalidates nominal
error” theorem. The contextual recommendation on pp.179-180 is not promoted into
a Protocol method-selection rule.

For Rasch et al., p.222 explicitly defines `gamma_2` as the standardized fourth
moment minus 3. Thus Table 1's value 15 is excess kurtosis 15. The packet's precision
repair is correct. It still refuses to assert equivalence to the unavailable earlier
presentation or a universal Welch policy.

The Lazic and Nosek statements are unchanged, bounded reuse: experimental unit is
not inferred from row count, and preregistration literature does not standardize or
prove the exact R5 timing/access-history representation. No new universal claim is
introduced.
