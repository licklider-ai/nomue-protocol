## Zimmerman 1997: bounded completion findings

### Z97-1: simulation scope

The paper's simulation compares independent-samples Student t tests and
paired-samples t tests using normally distributed data with induced correlation
between paired observations. Correlations range from -0.50 to +0.50, sample
sizes range from 10 to 80 pairs, one- and two-tailed tests use alpha .05, and
each reported simulation point uses 10,000 replications. The paper describes
the simulations as illustrative rather than exhaustive.

The reviewer inspected article pages 351-355, especially “Paired Data and
Nonindependence of Observations,” Figures 1-2 and Table 1. The quantitative
findings are bounded to the studied normal-population, induced-pairwise-
correlation and Student-t conditions. They are not a theorem about arbitrary
dependence, hierarchical clustering, arbitrary distributions or every
correlated-data problem. Any Release 5 claim that generalized these Type I
error or power results to dependence in general requires narrowing to the
studied paired and correlated two-sample setting.

### Z97-2: pairing is a design relationship, not a correlation classifier

The paper treats pairing as an existing correspondence in the design: repeated
observations on the same subjects, deliberate matching, natural pairing or
another known correspondence. Correlation is a property or consequence of that
paired structure and affects efficiency and error behavior. The paper does not
establish that an observed sample correlation by itself creates or proves the
pairing relationship.

The reviewer inspected pages 349-350 and 359-360. The source supports keeping
pair identity and repeated structure declarative and source-qualified. It does
not support a verifier inferring pairing merely because two columns are
correlated, nor does a low observed correlation establish independence.

### Z97-3: incorrect independence assumptions have qualified effects

Pinpoint: the reviewer return did not provide a more specific page, section,
figure or table pinpoint for this claim.

Within the paper's paired-data setup, applying an independent-samples t test to
correlated observations changes Type I error behavior. The reported paired test
remains near nominal .05, while the independent-samples test changes materially
as correlation changes. The direction is not a universal inflation rule: the
paper describes the significance function as spuriously elevated or depressed,
depending on correlation. The source does not support a generic claim that any
within-cluster correlation always inflates Type I error.

### Z97-4: reported matching ranges are not universal thresholds

Pinpoint: the reviewer return did not provide a more specific page, section,
figure or table pinpoint for this claim.

For the sample sizes studied, the paper reports that deliberate matching would
need correlations roughly in the .30-.40 range or higher to offset lost degrees
of freedom, while much smaller correlation can matter when observations are
already naturally paired. Those ranges are expressly tied to the investigated
sample sizes. They are not Protocol-level thresholds and must not be encoded as
cutoffs for deciding pairing, independence, repetition or admissibility.

### Z97-5: observed low correlation does not establish independence

Pinpoint: Table 2. The reviewer return did not provide a page or section
pinpoint for this claim.

The paper warns that even .10 or .15 correlation is not sufficient evidence of
independence in the investigated setup. That does not invert into a rule that a
measured correlation can establish dependence structure. The Table 2 example
starts from a hidden known correspondence of values; correlation is a property
of that pairing rather than the means by which pairing is discovered.

### Zimmerman 1997 disposition

**NARROWER CLAIM REQUIRED, then sufficient bounded evidence.** Pairing must not
be inferred from observed correlation, and the quantitative findings remain
tied to the paper's paired normal-data simulations. This finding supports a
narrower paired-data claim; it does not establish a universal clustering rule.

## Zimmerman 2004: bounded completion findings

### Z04-1: exact compound procedure

Pinpoint: the reviewer return did not provide a specific page, section, figure
or table pinpoint for this claim.

The simulated compound procedure first applies a Levene equality-of-variance
test. If Levene does not reject, it applies the pooled-variance Student
two-sample t test; if Levene rejects, it applies the Welch-Satterthwaite
separate-variance t test. The tests are non-directional. The study is not an
investigation of every method-selection process based on observed data.

### Z04-2: simulation conditions

Pinpoint: Tables 2 and 3 for the replication-count exception and the varied
preliminary-test significance level, respectively. The reviewer return did not
provide a page or section pinpoint for the remaining conditions.

The simulations use normally generated observations from two independent
samples. Population standard-deviation ratios range from 1 to 3; total sample
size is 30 or 60; and the `n1/n2` ratio ranges from 0.2 to 5. There are 50,000
replications per condition, except Table 2, which uses 200,000. Levene and the
location test are generally applied at .01 or .05; Table 3 varies the
preliminary-test significance level while holding the location-test level
fixed. The empirical claims must remain tied to this variance-pretest followed
by Student-or-Welch setting.

### Z04-3: bounded error-control finding

Pinpoint: the reviewer return did not provide a specific page, section, figure
or table pinpoint for this claim.

Under the simulated conditions, unconditional Welch stays near nominal while
the Levene-conditioned procedure can inherit distortion from Student and can
spoil Welch's otherwise favorable behavior. The result depends on sample-size
imbalance, population variance ratio and the preliminary-test threshold. The
paper reports severe distortion in some unequal-sample-size and unequal-
variance configurations and notes that distortion can be greater when variance
inequality is slight.

### Z04-4: no universal theorem

Pinpoint: Table 1 for the equal-variance boundary case. The reviewer return did
not provide a page or section pinpoint for the remaining limitations and
boundary cases.

The paper does not support the proposition that every outcome-dependent method
choice invalidates its nominal error rate. Its empirical analysis concerns the
specific Levene-to-Student-or-Welch procedure. Boundary cases also preclude an
“always invalid” reading: when variances are equal, Table 1 error rates are near
nominal; as a preliminary test's power approaches one under unequal variances,
the procedure approaches unconditional Welch use and the preliminary test
becomes pointless rather than necessarily distorting the result.

The bounded replacement claim is: same-data preliminary testing can make a
compound procedure's operating characteristics differ from the nominal
properties of its component tests, and Zimmerman 2004 demonstrates that result
for the studied Levene-to-Student-or-Welch procedure.

### Z04-5: unconditional Welch recommendation is contextual

Pinpoint: the reviewer return did not provide a specific page, section, figure
or table pinpoint for this claim.

The paper recommends unconditional separate-variance testing, particularly
with unequal sample sizes, and reports that Welch remains near nominal in its
simulations. That recommendation is bounded to the studied two-sample means
problem. It does not become a universal Protocol rule or authorize Release 5 to
choose statistical methods.

### Zimmerman 2004 disposition

**NARROWER CLAIM REQUIRED, then sufficient bounded evidence.** The paper's
recommendation for unconditional separate-variance testing is contextual to the
studied two-sample means problem. It does not become a universal Protocol rule,
does not authorize Release 5 to choose statistical methods, and does not prove a
theorem about all outcome-dependent method selection.

## Lazic 2010: comparison with the existing bounded claim

The supplied BMC article agrees with the existing addendum's bounded claims.
It defines the experimental unit as the smallest entity assignable to a
different treatment condition, distinguishes sample size from the number of
individual observations, and states that multiple measurements from one
experimental unit do not become independent samples because there are more
rows. It also distinguishes independence across subjects from nonindependence
within a subject and explains why cells within one rat cannot simply be counted
as independent replication.

This supports the bounded distinctions that an experimental unit is not an
arbitrary observation row, repeated measurements within one unit are not
independent replication, and nested or hierarchical observations can require
dependence-aware treatment. It does not establish universal Protocol vocabulary
or authorize a verifier to deduce the real experimental unit solely from a
string label or row count.

The 17-page PDF contains later signed responses by other authors. Their views
must not be silently merged into Lazic's claims. No material difference from
the existing bounded summary was identified. Disposition: **SUFFICIENT BOUNDED
EVIDENCE**.

## Rasch, Kubinger and Moder 2011: comparison with the existing bounded claim

The supplied Springer journal paper agrees with the existing addendum's bounded
claim and provides stronger reproducible support than the previously inspected
author presentation. It studies applying assumption pretests and a main test to
the same observations, which creates dependence among the statistics and makes
overall error behavior nontrivial.

The procedure is specific: Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests normality in each sample;
rejection selects Wilcoxon U; otherwise Levene tests variance homogeneity;
acceptance selects Student t and rejection selects Welch. The simulation
includes equal and unequal sample sizes, Fleishman nonnormal distributions,
skewness up to 3, kurtosis up to 15, heterogeneous variances, mean differences
from 0 to 5, and 100,000 generated sample pairs per condition.

The authors conclude that pretesting does not improve error behavior in the
examined context and recommend Welch as the default comparison test. They also
report exceptions where Welch performs worse than desired, especially the
extreme type-5 distribution and some very small-sample mixed-distribution
cases. The paper therefore does not support an unqualified theorem about all
same-data choices.

The earlier 11-page author presentation was not part of this completion-review
package, so byte- or paragraph-level equivalence was not tested. No material or
decision-bearing contradiction was identified between the publisher article
and the existing addendum's documented bounded summary. Disposition:
**SUFFICIENT BOUNDED EVIDENCE** for that documented claim; exact
presentation-to-journal textual equivalence remains untested.

## Nosek et al. 2018: comparison with the existing bounded claim

The supplied PNAS paper agrees with the existing bounded addendum when the
literature claim remains distinct from the proposed Release 5 convention. It
describes preregistration as committing to analytic steps without advance
knowledge of research outcomes and discusses direct and indirect prior exposure
for preexisting data, including who has seen the data, communicated summaries,
advice from previous analysts and knowledge about related variables.

This supports the bounded distinctions that a plan fixed without relevant
outcome knowledge differs from one formed after such knowledge, prior exposure
is not limited to opening the exact target column, and a preserved transparent
registration provides evidence unavailable from a bare after-the-fact
assertion. The paper does not standardize the exact Release 5 `pre_outcome`
enum, a universal supplied-dataset-wide boundary, dataset revision identity,
restoration or edit-history semantics, trusted timestamps, authorship or access
history proof, or access to other datasets. A bare enum does not prove those
facts.

No material difference from the existing bounded summary was identified.
Disposition: **SUFFICIENT BOUNDED EVIDENCE**, provided the exact Release 5
timing boundary remains identified as a Protocol convention and non-claim
rather than a literature-standardized rule.
