## 2. Executive disposition

- `FND1-H01` — **NARROW_AND_CLOSE**. Holm's procedure is fully specified in the primary text: ordering of obtained levels, thresholds `α/(n+1-k)`, stop at the first non-rejection, and rejection of all earlier-ordered hypotheses. Theorem 1 (printed p. 67) establishes the paper's free-combinations multiple-level condition, which is a strong family-wise statement over every non-empty set of true nulls, and its proof uses only the Boole inequality, so validity requires no dependence assumption on the test statistics beyond valid marginal levels. The paper also discusses two further constructions inside its own pages: a weighted variant (Theorem 2, printed p. 69) and an alternative product-form threshold set `1-(1-α)^(1/(n+1-k))` on printed p. 68, which is **independence-based**, not independence-free.
- `FND1-H02` — **NARROW_AND_CLOSE**. Benjamini and Hochberg define the error table, `V`, `R`, and `Q = V/(V+S)` with `Q` set to zero when there are no rejections, and define the false discovery rate as `E(Q) = E(V/R)` (printed p. 291). The step-up procedure is expression (1) on printed p. 293. Theorem 1 (printed p. 293) establishes false discovery rate control under independence of the test statistics, with the sharper bound `(m0/m)q* ≤ q*` completed in Appendix A (printed pp. 299–300), where the true-null p-values are handled as independent uniform variables. Positive-dependence, arbitrary-dependence, and adaptive-`m0` results are not in this paper.
- `FND1-H03` — **NARROW_AND_CLOSE**, with an explicitly retained successor evidence requirement. The direct-inspection evidence requirement is satisfied: both attached originals were inspected in full. The closure is narrowed to what the two texts actually support. Dunnett (1955) gives a general simultaneous-confidence construction for the many-to-one family at arbitrary group sizes through the multivariate-t correlation structure (equations (4) and (5), printed p. 1102; correlation formula, printed p. 1103), with supplied tables that are equal-group-size instruments. Tukey (1949) presents a three-stage gap, straggler, and F procedure and does **not** contain the later Studentized-range all-pairs procedure or the Tukey-Kramer extension; therefore the all-pairs half of the downstream deliverable named in the hold boundary is **not** sourced by these attachments and remains a separately defined evidence requirement against the correct later primary sources. Sections 6, 7, and 9 state this same boundary.

## 5. Paper-by-paper findings

### 5.1 Holm (1979), printed pp. 65–70

**Procedure.** Obtained significance levels are computed for each hypothesis, ordered ascending, and tested against the descending threshold series `α/n`, `α/(n-1)`, …, `α/1`. Rejection proceeds down the ordered list; at the first hypothesis whose level exceeds its threshold, that hypothesis and all subsequent ones are accepted and the procedure stops (Scheme 1, printed pp. 66–67).

**Error control.** Theorem 1 (printed p. 67) establishes the free-combinations multiple level of significance α as defined on printed p. 65. Because that definition quantifies over every non-empty candidate set of true nulls, the guarantee is a strong family-wise statement rather than a complete-null-only statement. The paper presents no weak-only alternative claim.

**Assumptions.** The proof uses the Boole inequality applied to the true-null index set, with `m = \|I\|` the number of true nulls. No dependence structure, exchangeability condition, or parametric family is required; the stated requirement is only that an obtained level can be computed for each separate test (printed p. 68).

**Constructions discussed inside the paper beyond Scheme 1.** Two are present and must not be described as absent. First, the product-form threshold series `1-(1-α)^(1/(n+1-k))` on printed p. 68, offered as slightly more powerful and presented for independent test statistics, hence independence-based. Second, the weighted variant of Scheme 2 with Theorem 2 on printed p. 69, again proved by the Boole inequality and therefore not requiring independence.

**Further properties.** Sequential rejection of this type is coherent and consonant by construction (printed p. 66). Inversion into a confidence set yields nothing tighter than classical Bonferroni (printed p. 68). Paired one-sided statements avoid contradiction under the sign and level conditions on printed p. 68.

**Not established here.** False discovery rate control; asymptotic arguments; validity of the product-form variant without independence.

### 5.2 Benjamini and Hochberg (1995), printed pp. 289–300

**Definitions (printed p. 291).** The error table fixes `m`, `m0`, `R`, `V`, `S`, `U`, and `T`, with only `R` observable. `Q` is the proportion of rejections that are false, set to zero when there are no rejections. The false discovery rate is the expectation of `Q`. Property (a) gives equality with the family-wise error rate under the complete null, hence weak family-wise control as a consequence. Property (b) gives the inequality in the other configurations, so family-wise control implies false discovery rate control but not conversely.

**Procedure (printed p. 293, expression (1)).** Order the p-values ascending; take the largest index whose ordered p-value is at most `(i/m)q*`; reject that hypothesis and all with smaller ordered p-values; reject nothing if no index qualifies.

**Theorem and proof scope.** Theorem 1 (printed p. 293) states control under independent test statistics and for any configuration of false nulls. The displayed proof yields `(m0/m)q* ≤ q*`, so the control level is bounded by `q*` and is tighter when some nulls are false. The supporting lemma (printed p. 293, inequality (2)) is proved by induction in Appendix A (printed pp. 299–300), where the true-null p-values are handled as independent uniform variables on the unit interval. The Remark on printed p. 293 records that independence among the false-null statistics is not needed.

**Related characterizations.** Theorem 2 (printed p. 295) identifies the procedure with a constrained maximization of the rejection count. Under the complete null the procedure controls the family-wise error rate at `q*` (printed p. 296). The paper records that a related step-up use of Simes's inequality fails strong family-wise control for some configurations, attributing that to Hommel (printed p. 293), and compares its own linear constants with Hochberg's constants (printed pp. 293–294).

**Evidence type for power.** Simulation with independent normal variables and z-tests (printed pp. 296–297), not an analytic power theorem.

**Not established here.** Control under positive dependence; control under arbitrary dependence; adaptive procedures using an estimator of `m0`; discrete or composite p-value settings; strong family-wise control.

### 5.3 Dunnett (1955), printed pp. 1096–1121

**Family and guarantee.** The family is the `p` treatment-minus-control differences (printed pp. 1096 and 1101). The guarantee stated is a joint confidence coefficient: all `p` interval statements are simultaneously correct with a preassigned probability. The paper does not restate this as a family-wise error rate for inverted tests; that reading is a downstream inference requiring a duality step absent from the text (ledger row D18).

**General construction at arbitrary group sizes.** Section III is general. Equations (4) and (5) on printed p. 1102 define the one-sided and two-sided constants through the joint law of the `p` t-statistics, with per-group sizes entering the standard errors, and the correlation expression on printed p. 1103 is a function of the group sizes rather than a fixed value. Equations (6) and (7) on printed p. 1103 reduce these to integrals of a multivariate normal distribution function against the density of the variance estimate. Section V (printed p. 1106, equation (12)) develops the unequal-allocation case explicitly, with a differing control size, and evaluates the achieved joint level numerically in Figs. 1 and 2. Accordingly the theoretical construction is **not** limited to equal group sizes, and this report does not state that it is.

**Instrument-level qualifications.** Tables 1a and 1b (printed p. 1105) are one-sided equicoordinate constants numerically evaluated for the correlation value one half, applicable where the control and all treatments have equal numbers of observations. The paper states that no approximation of the kind used for the two-sided tables was made for these, subject to the bracketing, three-point inverse interpolation, and reciprocal-degrees-of-freedom interpolation described in the same paragraph, with an accuracy claim of one unit in the second decimal place. Tables 2a and 2b are computed from the bivariate-t bound of equation (11) and are therefore conservative approximations even at equal group sizes; the parenthetical note on printed p. 1099 states that the tabulated two-sided constants are larger than necessary and the attained level slightly exceeds nominal.

**Reuse of the supplied tables under imbalance.** Applying the equal-group-size tables when group sizes are unequal achieves the intended level only approximately (printed pp. 1096 and 1099). This is a statement about reuse of those instruments, not a claim that every unequal-size construction available from Section III is approximate.

**Design and extension.** For joint levels near 0.95 and above, the control size is recommended at approximately the square root of the number of treatments times each treatment size (printed p. 1107). Section VI extends the approach to relative potencies against a common standard by Fieller's method (printed pp. 1107–1113).

**Not established here.** Exact equicoordinate tabulations for unequal group sizes; validity without normality or without a common variance; all-pairs protection; nonparametric many-to-one procedures.

### 5.4 Tukey (1949), printed pp. 99–114

**Family and inputs.** Comparison of individual treatment means within an analysis of variance, aiming to separate means into distinguishable groups and to detect residual heterogeneity (printed pp. 99–100). The inputs are observed treatment means and a common standard error of a single mean estimated independently from another line of the analysis-of-variance table on stated degrees of freedom (printed pp. 99–101). The paper's condition is that common, independently estimated standard error; it is not stated as an equal-sample-size requirement, and this report does not convert it into one.

**Three-stage procedure (printed pp. 102–104).** Stage one is a gap test on ordered adjacent means using two-sided Student t percentage points, adopted as an approximate and conservative standard. Stage two is a straggler test within groups using two-sided unit-normal percentage points applied to an approximated Studentized extreme deviate. Stage three is an F-test on the resulting groups or subgroups, and this component is an upper-tail variance-ratio test rather than a two-sided test, as the worked example on printed p. 104 shows by judging the F-ratio against an upper tail area.

**Error statement.** The paper does not establish an exact simultaneous guarantee. Printed p. 100 sets out a deliberately loose operational meaning for working at a stated level; printed p. 102 states that the frequency of false positives for the whole system is hard to obtain analytically and records the conjecture of a 1.2 to 1.6 times inflation over the per-stage level.

**Supporting evidence.** Sampling experiments and numerical integration (printed pp. 106 and 110–113, Tables 1 and 4 through 8) support the claim that largest-gap percentage points decrease with the number of means, making the two-mean t-based standard conservative. Printed pp. 107–108 assess the straggler approximation against external tabulations and supply a refinement for doubtful cases.

**Not contained here.** The Studentized-range all-pairs procedure later labelled the honestly significant difference method; the Tukey-Kramer unequal-size extension; any exact all-pairs protected family with simultaneous coverage. The Studentized-range approach appears only as Newman's cited comparator (printed pp. 102 and 104–105).

## 6. Cross-source boundary findings

### 6.1 Do many-to-one, all-pairs, and sequential procedures define different protected objects and guarantee boundaries?

The four attachments define three distinct protected objects, and the all-pairs object is **not** among them.

- Dunnett (1955) protects the many-to-one family of treatment-minus-control differences with a joint confidence coefficient, constructed generally through the multivariate-t correlation structure at arbitrary group sizes (printed pp. 1101–1103, 1106) and instrumented by equal-group-size tables (printed p. 1105).
- Tukey (1949) addresses within-analysis-of-variance grouping of treatment means by a three-stage gap, straggler, and upper-tail F procedure whose composite error rate is conjectural (printed pp. 100–104). It does not define an exact all-pairs protected family.
- Holm (1979) protects an abstract set of `n` hypotheses against every candidate true-null configuration, requiring only valid marginal levels (printed pp. 65–68).
- Benjamini and Hochberg (1995) act on the same kind of abstract hypothesis set but control a different error functional (Section 6.2).

Guarantee boundaries differ correspondingly: a joint confidence coefficient under normality and a common variance with an independent variance estimate (Dunnett); a conjectural composite false-positive frequency with no exact simultaneous claim (Tukey 1949); a strong family-wise guarantee with no dependence assumption (Holm); a false discovery rate guarantee under independence (Benjamini and Hochberg).

Consequently, an all-pairs simultaneous guarantee is not obtainable from these four texts. Any such guarantee must be sourced from the later Studentized-range and unequal-size literature, which is outside the attachments and outside this commission.

### 6.2 Do the family-wise error rate and the false discovery rate remain different quantities for the same hypothesis set?

Yes, and the distinction is established directly in the primary text for one and the same set of `m` hypotheses (Benjamini and Hochberg, printed p. 291):

- the family-wise error rate is the probability that at least one true null is rejected; the false discovery rate is the expectation of `V/R` with the convention that this is zero when there are no rejections;
- the two coincide under the complete null and otherwise the false discovery rate is the smaller quantity;
- family-wise control implies false discovery rate control at the same level, and the converse fails;
- the step-up procedure controls the false discovery rate (printed p. 293) and controls the family-wise error rate only under the complete null configuration (printed p. 296).

### 6.3 Which original-paper conditions must attach to any future procedure identity?

- Holm (1979): ordering of obtained levels; thresholds `α/(n+1-k)`; stop at first non-rejection; validity from marginal levels alone with no dependence assumption; the free-combinations, that is strong, family-wise reading. If the product-form thresholds of printed p. 68 are used instead, the independence condition attaches. If the weighted Scheme 2 form is used, the importance constants and Theorem 2's proportional allocation attach.
- Benjamini and Hochberg (1995): the `(i/m)q*` step-up thresholds; step-up scan from the largest ordered p-value; false discovery rate rather than family-wise error rate as the controlled functional; independence of the true-null p-values as the proof condition; the achieved bound `(m0/m)q* ≤ q*`; family-wise control only under the complete null.
- Dunnett (1955): the many-to-one comparison family; normality with a common variance and an independent variance estimate on stated degrees of freedom; the multivariate-t correlation structure determined by the group sizes; sidedness-specific constants; and, where the paper's tables are used, the equal-group-size scope of Tables 1a and 1b and the conservative bivariate-t basis of Tables 2a and 2b. A family-wise error restatement is a downstream inference, not a statement of this text.
- Tukey (1949): the specific three-stage gap, straggler, and upper-tail F sequence; two-sided critical values in the gap and straggler stages only; a common independently estimated standard error of the means; and the conjectural, not exact, composite error statement.

Downstream constructions that rely on properties absent from these texts must be attributed to their own primary sources: the Studentized-range all-pairs procedure; the Tukey-Kramer unequal-size extension; dependence-corrected and adaptive false discovery rate procedures; heteroscedastic or nonparametric many-to-one procedures; and later formalizations or software realizations of Holm's weighted and product-form variants.

### 6.4 Which claims from the prior reconciliation must remain held?

From the reconciled result's Section 9.2, the holds outside this commission remain held and receive no findings here: `FND1-H04`, `FND1-H05`, `FND1-H06`, `FND1-H07`, and `FND1-H08`. In addition, within the `FND1-H03` boundary, the all-pairs half of the named downstream semantics remains unsupported by the attachments and is retained as a separately defined evidence requirement against the later Studentized-range and unequal-size primary sources, as stated identically in Sections 2, 7, and 9.
