## 7. Bearer, condition, time, summary, and observability analysis

**Bearer.** `PO-01` gives a physical bearer (a plot); `PO-03` gives an abstract bearer (a unit, with subjects, laboratory equipment, households, and plots offered as concrete examples); `PO-02` gives a compound bearer, the trial, defined as a unit paired with an initiation time `t1` and a measurement time `t2` with `t1 < t2` (p. 689). No single text defines a domain-general bearer that is at once physical and time-indexed by construction.

**Condition.** `PO-01`'s conditions are agricultural varieties (extended to fertilizers on p. 472); `PO-02`'s conditions are two mutually-exclusive treatments `E` and `C` with an exclusivity requirement stated per trial (p. 689, assumption (b)); `PO-03`'s conditions are two causes `t` and `c` recorded by `S(u)`, deliberately generic (p. 946). None of the three texts uses ICH E9(R1) intercurrent-event vocabulary; such vocabulary did not exist at the time of writing.

**Time / assessment occasion.** `PO-01` does not index outcomes by time. `PO-02` makes both `t1` and `t2` constitutive of the definition of the effect (p. 689) and additionally requires that `t1` be ascertainable per trial (assumption (a) on the same page). `PO-03` splits variables into ones measured before the applied cause and ones measured after (p. 946), and states two named assumptions covering response invariance in the applied-cause time and non-carryover of prior exposure (p. 948). A project claim that the potential-outcomes lineage generically supplies an assessment-occasion element is grounded directly in `PO-02` and `PO-03`; for `PO-01` it is only implicit.

**Population-level summary.** All three texts define at least one population-level summary of per-bearer values, and all three take an arithmetic mean over bearers as the leading choice: `a_i` and `a_i − a_j` in `PO-01`; `τ` on p. 692 (2N-trial study) and `T` on p. 697 (M-trial generalization population) in `PO-02`; `E(Y_t − Y_c) = E(Y_t) − E(Y_c)` in `PO-03`. Only `PO-02` explicitly considers alternative summaries (median, midmean) in a footnote on p. 690 and explicitly rejects them for tractability under randomization; only `PO-03` explicitly discusses the interpretive cost of using an average when per-unit contrasts vary appreciably (§4.4, p. 949).

**Observability.** `PO-01` records the per-plot yield as observed for one variety only (p. 465); `PO-02` states directly that both `y(E)` and `y(C)` cannot be observed for the same trial because we cannot return to `t1` to give the other treatment (p. 689); `PO-03` names the impossibility of jointly observing both per-unit potential responses as fundamental (p. 947). None of the three texts calls the unobserved per-bearer outcome "missing" inside its definitional core; each treats it as an object that is defined and unobserved rather than an object that is deleted or absent. This is consistent with the accepted-boundary finding on the distinction between an outcome that remains defined but unobserved and an outcome that is deleted or absent.

## 8. Falsification cases

Each case records bearer, unit set, condition index, time relation, summary, target-identity effect, estimator effect, source IDs, missing declarations, and whether the candidate survives. Numerical equality is never treated as semantic identity. Any constructed extension beyond what an inspected source actually supplies is graded `CROSS_SOURCE_INFERENCE` or `POSSIBLE_PROJECT_CONVENTION`.

**Case 1 — same plots and yield scale, a different pair of varieties.**
Bearer: plot. Unit set: same finite field `U_1, …, U_m`. Condition index: `i, j` replaced by `i′, j′`. Time relation: same harvest. Summary: `a_{i′} − a_{j′}`. Target-identity effect: the target quantity changes but the target structure (a contrast between two per-variety means over the same finite field) is unchanged. Estimator effect: the same difference-of-averages estimator applies; the same variance formula (Eq. (17), p. 471) applies. Source IDs: `PO-01`. Missing declarations: none. **Candidate survives.**

**Case 2 — same observed sample, a different declared target unit set.**
Bearer: plot / unit / trial. Unit set: a declared `U′` different from the sampled `U` (or, in `PO-02`'s wording, a different `M`-population from the one the `2N` were drawn from). Condition index: same. Time relation: same. Summary: `a_i(U′)` or an analogous `T(M′)`. Target-identity effect: the target quantity is a different number even though the observed sample is unchanged. Estimator effect: the design-based estimator that was unbiased for the original target does not become an estimator of the new target without an additional generalization argument. `PO-02` makes this dependence explicit through the assumed-random-sampling reasoning on pp. 698-699 and by naming distinct symbols `τ` and `T` on distinct pages. `PO-03` (§2, p. 945) makes probabilities and expected values population-quantities computed over `U`, so a redefinition of `U` redefines the target. Source IDs: `PO-01`, `PO-02`, `PO-03`. Missing declarations: an explicit target-unit-set declaration is required to prevent an identity error. **Candidate survives and is reinforced.**

**Case 3 — same per-bearer potential outcomes, a different population summary.**
Bearer: unit / trial. Unit set: same. Condition index: same. Time relation: same. Summary: replace the arithmetic mean by a median (considered in `PO-02`'s p. 690 footnote 6), a midmean (also p. 690 footnote 6), a conditional mean `T(z)` (`PO-03`'s Eq. (35), p. 958), or a population variance of per-unit contrasts. Target-identity effect: the same per-bearer potential outcomes support a family of distinct summaries; each is a distinct target quantity as a mathematical object. Estimator effect: different estimators (mean, median, conditional mean, variance) apply. Source IDs: `PO-01`, `PO-02`, `PO-03`. Missing declarations: the summary functional must be declared as part of the target. **Candidate survives and is reinforced;** the population-level-summary element must be read broadly enough to admit summaries other than an arithmetic mean.

**Case 4 — a descriptive single-condition mean for which no contrast is requested.**
Bearer: unit / trial. Unit set: same. Condition index: single condition. Time relation: fixed. Summary: `a_i` in `PO-01` (per-variety mean, p. 467); `E(Y_t)` in `PO-03` (§4.4, p. 949, treats a single-condition population mean as of practical interest). `PO-02` treats a contrast as constitutive of its per-trial cause-versus-cause difference and of both `τ` and `T`, so its own vocabulary does not license a non-contrastive target. Target-identity effect: a well-defined single-condition target exists in `PO-01` and `PO-03` but not in `PO-02`. Estimator effect: the per-variety mean estimator (Eq. (16), p. 469) in `PO-01`, and the design-based estimator of `E(Y_t)` in `PO-03`. Source IDs: `PO-01`, `PO-03`. Missing declarations: none within `PO-01` and `PO-03`; `PO-02` would need an added declaration to admit a non-contrastive target. **Candidate survives in its conditional form** ("a comparison direction is conditional on a comparison existing") and is directly supported by `PO-01` and `PO-03`.

**Case 5 — interference: one unit's outcome depends on another unit's assigned condition.**
Bearer: unit / trial. Unit set: same. Condition index: joint condition vector over all bearers; the per-bearer `Y_t(u)` is no longer a function of `u` alone. Time relation: fixed. Summary rewritten with a joint-assignment index — but this rewriting is a construction outside every inspected text and is `CROSS_SOURCE_INFERENCE`. What the inspected texts directly establish: `PO-01`'s urn model works with a doubly-indexed array `U_{ik}` that assigns one yield per (plot, variety) pair without reference to other plots' assignments; `PO-02` defines the per-trial `y(E)` and `y(C)` as functions of a single trial and does not consider between-trial dependence; `PO-03` defines `Y_t(u)` and `Y_c(u)` per unit and, in §4.3, imposes an independence condition on the assignment mechanism, not a between-units no-interference condition. Consequently: under interference, the notation of every inspected text becomes inadequate — the per-bearer potential-outcome pairs are no longer well-defined single objects — and the identification argument that runs from the design-based estimator to the population target requires an added between-units condition that no inspected text supplies. No inspected text directly establishes that any specific estimator is biased under interference; the direct finding is that the target and its identification argument stop being well-defined. Source IDs: `PO-01`, `PO-02`, `PO-03`. Missing declarations: an explicit interference-scope declaration. **Candidate survives conditionally**: the four principal elements remain necessary but are insufficient; any project addition here is graded `POSSIBLE_PROJECT_CONVENTION`.

**Case 6 — multiple versions of a nominally identical condition.**
Bearer: unit / trial. Condition index: nominal condition `t` with hidden sub-versions `t^{(1)}, t^{(2)}, …`; per-bearer potential outcomes differ across sub-versions. Time relation: fixed. Summary: depends on which sub-version is applied. What the inspected texts directly establish: `PO-01` handles this only in the closed setting of one variety per urn; `PO-03` treats operational definition of a cause philosophically (Example B, §7, pp. 954-955) but does not name a treatment-version condition; `PO-02` states a per-trial exclusivity condition on `E` and `C` (p. 689, assumption (b)) and a pre-treatment covariate invariance condition requiring the covariate to take the same value under `E` as under `C` (p. 696 with footnote 8). The `PO-02` covariate-invariance condition is per-covariate and per-trial and does not directly address multiple treatment versions; it is recorded here only as adjacent invariance evidence. Any reading of that condition as a treatment-version condition is `CROSS_SOURCE_INFERENCE` at best, and any project extension that promotes it to a lineage-supplied treatment-version condition is `POSSIBLE_PROJECT_CONVENTION`. Consequently: without a version-specification, the notation `y(E)`, `Y_t(u)` is not a well-defined single value, and the identification argument requires an added treatment-version declaration that no inspected text supplies. Source IDs: `PO-01`, `PO-02`, `PO-03`. **Candidate survives conditionally.**

**Case 7 — investigator-created counterexample: candidate target = population variance of per-unit contrasts.**
Bearer: unit. Unit set: `U`. Condition index: `t`, `c`. Time relation: fixed. Summary: `Var(Y_t(u) − Y_c(u))`. This quantity is not itself a signed population-level contrast, although it is a functional of per-unit contrasts; the inspected texts do not classify it as a target. `PO-03` §4.4 (p. 949) notes that when the variability of per-unit contrasts is large the population mean may not represent the effect for a specific unit well, but does not lift `Var(Y_t − Y_c)` to a target-quantity role. `PO-01` and `PO-02` do not consider such a summary. Consequently: the candidate's population-level-summary element must be read broadly enough to admit such a summary if a project wishes to use one, and the candidate is silent on how to choose the summary functional. Any project claim that this summary is or is not a target quantity is `POSSIBLE_PROJECT_CONVENTION` — the inspected texts do not decide it. **Candidate survives with a narrowing note.**

## 9. Cross-source agreements and material disagreements

**Agreements.**

- All three texts treat causal or target quantities as defined on a population of bearers (plots for `PO-01`, trials for `PO-02`, units for `PO-03`) and take a population-level summary — typically a mean or mean-difference — as the object estimators approximate.
- All three texts insist that only one per-bearer response is observed per bearer per pass: `PO-01` at the per-plot yield level; `PO-02` as an impossibility of both `y(E)` and `y(C)` for the same trial because we cannot return to `t1` (p. 689); `PO-03` as the impossibility of jointly observing both potential responses on the same unit, named as fundamental (p. 947).
- All three texts describe an assignment or sampling mechanism separately from the target quantity. The target quantity is defined before any assignment or estimator.
- None of the three texts calls the unobserved per-bearer outcome "missing" inside its definitional core.

These agreements are recorded as convergent, separately authored, and separately issued direct findings; source count is not treated as a vote and no agreement is used to elevate the status of a claim that lacks a direct pinpoint in the individual source.

**Material disagreements, preserved not collapsed.**

- **Whether time is constitutive of the bearer.** `PO-01` does not index outcomes by time. `PO-02` makes both `t1` and `t2` constitutive of the bearer (the trial), so the bearer itself carries the time indices. `PO-03` treats time as an active variable through the before/after-cause split and two named time-related assumptions, but does not fold time into the bearer.
- **Whether a contrast is constitutive of the target.** `PO-02` treats a contrast as constitutive (per-trial cause-versus-cause difference; `τ` and `T` defined as averages of such differences, pp. 689-697). `PO-01` and `PO-03` both define non-contrastive per-condition means alongside the contrasts (Eq. (16) in `PO-01`; §4.4 in `PO-03`). The strong reading "every target quantity in the lineage is a contrast" is therefore contradicted by `PO-01` and `PO-03`; the candidate's own wording — "a comparison direction is conditional on a comparison existing" — is consistent with all three texts.
- **Bearer level of abstraction.** `PO-01` requires a physical bearer (a plot); `PO-03` deliberately abstracts the bearer to any unit; `PO-02` requires a bearer that carries an initiation time and a measurement time (the trial). A project claim that "the lineage supplies a domain-general bearer definition" cannot be sourced to any single text without loss.
- **Where the covariate condition sits.** `PO-02` (p. 696 with footnote 8) states a per-covariate invariance condition tying the pre-treatment covariate value to both `E` and `C` and links it directly to unbiasedness over the allocation set; `PO-03` (§4.3, p. 948) uses conditional independence in the same role via strong ignorability referenced through later work; `PO-01` does not treat this problem. This is a genuine content-level disagreement about the definitional role of pre-cause variables and is preserved.

## 10. Overclaim, transfer, and misattribution attacks

- **Overclaim attack 1 — that Neyman 1990 defines the modern category "target quantity".** Neyman's own objects are per-variety means and their difference (pp. 466-472); the modern general category is not used by that name. Any mapping is `POSSIBLE_PROJECT_CONVENTION` (row N11).
- **Overclaim attack 2 — that Rubin 1974 defines the modern category "target quantity".** Rubin's own objects are the per-trial contrast, `τ` for the `2N`-trial study, `T` for the `M`-trial generalization population, and a hypothesized average used for significance levels (pp. 689-695, 697). Any mapping to the modern general category is `POSSIBLE_PROJECT_CONVENTION` (row R15).
- **Overclaim attack 3 — that Holland 1986 defines SUTVA.** Holland does not define, name, or use "SUTVA" or a between-units no-interference assumption in his own naming; his named assumptions cover assignment independence, cross-unit response identity, invariance in the applied-cause time, non-carryover of prior exposure, and identical per-unit effects, each named separately in §§4.1-4.4. A claim that the lineage supplies SUTVA is `ABSENT_IN_INSPECTED_SCOPE` for all three inspected texts (rows N9, R14, H8), and the SUTVA acronym belongs to later work outside the commissioned scope.
- **Overclaim attack 4 — that Rubin 1974 defines SUTVA or a between-units no-interference condition.** The per-trial exclusivity of `E` and `C` (p. 689) and the same-value-under-`E`-as-under-`C` covariate-invariance condition (p. 696 with footnote 8) are per-trial or per-covariate conditions and do not constitute a between-units no-interference condition.
- **Overclaim attack 5 — that Rubin 1974's covariate condition directly addresses multiple treatment versions.** The condition is stated for pre-cause covariates, not for versions of the treatments. Adjacent invariance evidence at most; a treatment-version reading is `CROSS_SOURCE_INFERENCE` or `POSSIBLE_PROJECT_CONVENTION`.
- **Overclaim attack 6 — that Rubin 1974 uses a single `T` symbol for the summary of interest throughout the paper.** The paper uses Greek lowercase `τ` on p. 692 for the summary over the `2N` in-study trials and capital Latin `T` on p. 697 for the summary over the `M`-trial generalization population; the p. 697 formulation states unbiasedness for `T` and, in a parenthetical, denies unbiasedness for `τ`. Any project use that identifies these two symbols is a `POSSIBLE_PROJECT_CONVENTION`.
- **Transfer attack — that any of the three texts define animal or humane-endpoint vocabulary, terminal-event handling, or post-assignment condition change.** None does. `PO-01` is agricultural; `PO-02` uses educational and physical-science examples; `PO-03` uses medical, economic, and social-science examples but does not define terminal-event handling or animal-endpoint vocabulary.
- **Misattribution attack — that "unit-treatment additivity" is Neyman's own phrase.** The phrase appears only in an editorial bracketed note on p. 472 of the 1990 translation; Neyman's own text records the operational choice `r = 1` without using that modern label.
- **Interpretation-of-absence attack.** The absence of SUTVA in Holland's inspected pages and the absence of an explicit assessment-occasion index in Neyman's inspected pages are absences of particular words and constructions in specified inspected ranges; they are not absences of the underlying concept. Rubin's per-trial exclusivity condition (p. 689) and Holland's assumptions in §§4.1-4.4 each perform some of the work later concentrated in the SUTVA label; this report does not deny that and does not backdate the label.
